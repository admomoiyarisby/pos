## Assessment A: Design Review

### AI Slop Verdict

**Clean.** This does NOT look AI-generated. The interface is clearly purpose-built for a specific operational domain (ghost kitchen POS/inventory). It makes deliberate choices: Indonesian-first labels, system font stack (no custom fonts), restrained monochrome palette, flat surfaces, dense data tables. The product register constraints are respected: no decorative motion, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no hero-metric dashboards, no illustrated food icons. The "restaurant SaaS" clichés are absent. This looks like a tool built by people who understand the job.

The category-reflex check also passes: it's not the warm-neutral SaaS cream of 2026, it's a cool-gray neutral with a clear structural color system. And it's not the "not-saaS → editorial typographic" reflex either — the tool leans into density and operational utility, not white space and display typography.

**Cognitive Load Score: 1 failure → Low.** The table action columns are the primary concern. A user confronted with 5+ action buttons per row must visually scan and parse each icon/label before selecting.

### Emotional Journey

**Path**: Login → Main task starts directly → Data/forms → Success → Next task

The emotional arc is flat by design ("Calm" is a brand principle) — and that's correct for a serious operational tool. No celebration animations, no confetti on a completed transaction. The POS success modal is appropriately sober: check icon, order details, print button, new transaction. The auto-close at 4 seconds is good for throughput.

**Risk**: The consistency gap between the two modal systems could subtly erode trust. A user who encounters different-looking dialogs in different parts of the app may feel the system is less polished, even if they can't articulate why.
